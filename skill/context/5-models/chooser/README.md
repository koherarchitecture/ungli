## Files

- `vocab.py` makes `words.json`, every word of the Museum's visible pages that passes the rule in its docstring (13,326 on 10 November 2026), and `sets.json`, 1000 sets of 1000 different words drawn at random with a fixed seed, so each word is in about 75 sets.
- `names.json` and `names.py`: no names to choose from (his word, 10 November 2026: *"nachiket is proper noun, indian name - replace all names"*). `names.json` lists 107 names found in the Museum's texts (people, places, peoples and languages, brands, deities and planets, days and months, and the characters of his fiction such as Peela Khargosh, Hoja Asli and the Gurks); an ordinary English word the Museum also uses as a name (regal, hem, banner) stays a word. `names.py` swaps every name for a word that is not a name, in every set not yet used by a saved poem. Edit `names.json` and run `names.py` again to take out more. `veganise.json` (10 November 2026, his word: *"run the veganise filter on all sets"*) is the veganise agent's reading of all 13,326 words: 39 words that, standing alone, treat animal use as normal (animal products such as milk, cheese, silk; killing and catching such as cull, hunting; breeding; zoo and pets; insults through animals such as vermin, brute), each with the rule it breaks; its borderline words (bred, hunt, farm, herd, tame, wild, stray and others) were kept and are listed in its report of that day for him to decide. `names.py` takes out both lists, replacing every removed word with a word in neither list so each set keeps 1000; it ran from set 18 first (2,895 words replaced in sets 18 to 999), then, on his word the same day (*"the poems done are done - all sets from 0 to 17 should be veganised now"*), over every set, replacing 70 more words in sets 0 to 17, the 20 names in sets 0 to 2 among them. So the sets of poems 1 to 17 are no longer exactly what he saw: they now hold 1000 words with none from either list. A word a saved poem chose is never replaced (none of the 17 poems had chosen a listed word), so every poem's choices stay among its set's 1000 for training. After `names.py` runs, reload keyer's page, since the open set may have changed. `vocab.py` refuses to run once any poem is saved, because remaking the sets would break the poems' record.
- `marks.json`: the punctuation marks he may place in a slot in place of a word (his word, 10 November 2026: *"allow punctuation marks to be added in place of a word"*), offered in every set and usable more than once in a poem. `cipher.py` gives each mark a code no word has, so the model learns marks as it learns words, and `rows.py` adds them to every set's codes (1013 a set with the 13 marks of that day). Add new marks only at the end of the list, since a mark's code depends on its place.
- `cipher.py` gives each word a fixed five-digit code, shuffled by the secret in `cipher.key` (made on first run, never to be changed once poems are encrypted), and turns a code back into its word. Five digits because three can name only 1000 things and the pool holds 13,326.
- `poems.jsonl` is his keys: each line one poem he composed in `keyer`, with the number of the set it was made from. Nothing else writes to it. He can rewrite a saved poem from keyer's Poems panel; the line is changed in place and keeps its edit history under `earlier`, every earlier version as `{"poem", "written"}` with the time it was saved, and the time of the latest rewrite under `edited` (his word, 10 November 2026: *"edit history for each poem saved"*). Training reads only the current version (`poem`); the earlier versions are a record of the meaning being built, kept for him. A poem he marks special carries `"special": true` (poem 15, set 14, marked on his word on 10 November 2026: *"mark poem 15 as special"*). What the mark means for training has not been said, so `rows.py` and the training ignore it until he says.
- `rows.py` turns the poems into `set-codes.json` (every set as codes) and `rows.jsonl` (fifteen rows a poem: set, slot, codes chosen before, the code chosen).
- `baseline.py` scores plain counting: how often he chose a word after the previous one, and at all. The mean rank of his real choice among the set's 1000 on the held-out last fifth of the poems; chance is about 500.
- `model.py`, `train.py`, `choose.py` are the chooser, its training and its choosing, run on `gpubox` in the shared venv `[path withheld]`. Keep the model only if `train.py`'s mean rank beats `baseline.py`'s on the same rows.
- `poem.py` decrypts a line from `choose.py` into the poem, on the Mac.

## Making the training data

On the Mac, press Cmd-Space and type *Write poems*: the app `[path withheld] poems.app` starts `keyer` and opens the page at http://127.0.0.1:8797/#chooser-poems, or just opens the page if `keyer` is already running. The app lives outside Dropbox because Dropbox's File Provider strips the execute bit from launchers inside it; it is only a launcher, rebuilt with the `osacompile` line in `run.txt`, and holds nothing that needs syncing. The ThinkPad has no launcher yet; there, run `python3 [path withheld] chooser-poems`.

The page is `keyer`'s deck *Chooser: poems from the Museum's words*. It shows one set of 1000 words in a shuffled order, fixed for that set, with no suggestions. Poems 1 to 6 were made from alphabetical lists, and 40 of their 84 pairs of neighbouring words sat within 30 places of each other in the list (about 5 would be chance), so the list recorded his scrolling as much as his choosing; the model cannot see list order, since the codes are shuffled. From poem 7 the list is shuffled (his word, 10 November 2026: *"shuffled"*), and each such poem carries `"order": "shuffled"`; a poem without it was made from an alphabetical list. Type to narrow the list, Tab moves the selection through the words shown (Shift-Tab back), Enter places the selected word (or click a word), and the fifteen slots fill six, three, six. Enter saves the full poem; the next poem gets the next set, so the sets keep switching. Backspace in the empty box takes back the last word, or the last saved poem. Esc clears the poem.

Each poem gives fifteen rows. With fifty poems the model mostly learns which words he favours in which slot and which pairs he puts together; a few hundred is where it can start to learn his way of choosing. The baseline says how much of that counting already does.

## Four versions, numbered by semver

His plan, 10 November 2026: *"i will do 200 in tranches of 50 - four versions of models"*, numbered by semantic versioning (his word: *"swmver!"*). The minor number rises with each tranche of poems: 0.1.0 is trained at 50 poems, 0.2.0 at 100, 0.3.0 at 150, 0.4.0 at 200, each on every poem keyed so far. The patch number rises when a version is retrained on the same poems after a fix to the code (0.1.1). 1.0.0 is kept for the version he says works; until then everything is 0.x. Each version keeps its own folder, `versions/0.1.0/` and so on, holding `chooser.pt`, `chooser-recent.pt` (the same version trained on the recent half only), `train.log` and `baseline.txt`, so no version overwrites another. Each is scored against the counting baseline on its own held-out last fifth. At 200, all four are also scored on the same poems, the last 40 (161 to 200), which none of them trained on, so the versions can be compared with each other directly.

## His poems change as he goes

His words, 10 November 2026: *"I am trying to construct meaning so as we progress - progessive poems will be better"*. The poems are not a steady sample of one way of choosing: he is finding the meaning as he writes, so later poems are meant to be better than earlier ones. Three things follow for the versions, as read by Claude and his to correct (the second adopted by him the same day: *"do as you propose"*). The held-out fifth is always the latest poems, so each version is scored on where his choosing has got to, not where it started; a score that falls from one version to the next may mean his choosing has moved past what the earlier poems taught, not that the model got worse. At each version, a second model is trained on the most recent half of the poems only (about 10 seconds on gpubox) and scored on the same held-out fifth, so his own data says whether the early poems help or hold the model back; the weighting itself stays equal until he states a rule. His *special* marks are a second key and stay unused in training until he says what they are for.
