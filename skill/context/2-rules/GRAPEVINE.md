## The rule over all the others

0. **Never change too fast.** One change at a time, judged on the mean over three pointing seeds across at least two rounds, never on one round; a change expected to dip first (a re-warm, a new head) is judged after the dip. A recipe changed on every reading has no direction. On 24–25 Sep the changes that held were structural (pointing in pretraining, the mixed text, more text) and the ones that failed were schedule changes made on single draws. *(decided 25 Sep 2026)*

0a. **One run has one driver.** On 25 Sep `mom-big-3`'s recipe was changed and the run restarted (11:02) from one place while another was watching it on the old recipe; the watcher read the soup's anneal as a bug and quoted round 2 as 120 when the run counted it as 164. Before changing, stopping or reading a run, check who is driving it and what they last changed; if it is not you, leave it alone. *(25 Sep, 11:09)*

## Reading the numbers

1. **The count is taken at the threshold that lets no non-answer through, so one confident non-answer can zero it.** The 75M arm counted 0 at 3,000 steps while its median honest margin improved from −18.8 to −10.5; round 1 of the pruned run was held to 3 by a single off-question answer at 12.84 (*"What were you carrying?" / "Under the flyover."*). Never read one count alone. *(24 Sep)*
2. **Look at the margins, not only the count.** Two numbers explain a count: where the honest answers' margins sit (their median) and the single highest margin among non-answers (it is the threshold). Every round records both: `median_honest_margin` and `worst_non_answer` in each set's results (the second from 24 Sep, 20:45). *(24 Sep)*
3. **Bias is not the problem; separation is.** Pushing the reader toward or away from "nothing" only moves the threshold. Cutting rai's no-answer rows from 49% to 25% took round 2's base from 71 to 15, because it found spans in more non-answers and the threshold rose from 6.76 to 12.00. Only a reader that reads better widens the gap between answers and non-answers. *(24 Sep)*
4. ~~The reader is starved: it refuses almost everything.~~ Wrong, and overturned by lesson 3 the same evening. The honest margins were deep on the "nothing" side, but moving them there or back does not change the count. *(24 Sep)*
5. ~~**A gap under about 20 counts is a tie.**~~ The pointing stage alone moved one base from 53 to 147 on 24 Sep. *(24 Sep)* Too narrow; see 5a.
5a. **Neighbouring rounds of one run swing by 50 or more.** `mom-mix-1`'s anneal went 103, 158, 101 over three rounds, and rounds 5–8 went 97, 151, 143, 134. One count is one draw: judge a run by the best of several rounds or by the trend of the margins, and before trusting any single number, measure its spread by pointing the same base again with other seeds. *(25 Sep, about 05:00)*
5b. **The pointing seed alone moves a count by about 30.** Round 10's base, pointed with seeds 7, 1, 2, 3: 158, 127, 148, 124. Artwork and unseen barely move (14–16, 29–34); v1 and the threshold carry the swing. Report a base as the mean over seeds, never as one pointing; `rai.train --seed` exists for this (25 Sep). *(25 Sep, about 05:05)*
5c. **A stopping rule that reads one draw stops on luck; score every round as a mean.** `mom-big-1` went 163 then 116 on single pointings while its margin barely moved (+12.3, +11.2), and round 4's second seed counted 91. From 25 Sep, 06:45 `clean_base.py` points each round's base with seeds 7, 1 and 2 and records their mean and each total (`totals_by_seed`); about 7 minutes a round. Rounds scored before then are compared only after their own seeds are added. *(25 Sep, 06:50)*

## What the pretraining needs

6. **Masked-language training alone does not teach pointing.** Rounds 1 to 4 of the first clean base counted 32, 80, 53, 78 of 300 while its loss fell from 5.94 to 4.18. A falling loss is not a reader. *(24 Sep)*
7. **Teach pointing during pretraining.** With span-selection rows beside the MLM rows from step 0 (Ram et al. 2021, Glass et al. 2020), round 2 counted 71 at 3,280 steps, where the recipe without them needed 9,896 steps for 80. *(24 Sep)*
8. **Early rounds count almost nothing; that is not a verdict.** Round 1 of the pruned run counted 3 at 1,640 steps and round 2 counted 71 at 3,280. Warm-up is 2,000 steps; nothing before the learning rate peaks says much. *(24 Sep)*

8a. **Before calling an early count expected, set it beside an earlier run at the same tokens read.** Lesson 8 covers the steps before warm-up ends, nothing after. `mom-large-1`'s round 1 counted 37 at 9,000 steps and was reported as lesson 8; `mom-big-1` had counted 163 at the same 9,000 steps and tokens, ~~and the wider base's held-out loss was lower (5.947 against 6.41)~~ (struck 20:45: the two held-out losses are not taken the same way; the training losses are comparable and put the wide base behind, 5.45 against 4.84 at 9,000 steps, because its peak rate is a fifth of `mom-big-1`'s). The gap was a finding, and it waited for Prayas to ask (*"why should I have to ask if a score is too low? you should figure, tell me and fix"*). Every round's report now carries its matched comparison. *(25 Sep, 20:30)*
9. **The pointing stage's settings matter less than the base.** A gentler pointing stage (2 passes at 5e-5) on round 1's base counted 1 against 3. Fix the base, not the pointing. *(24 Sep)*
10. **It needs hours, not minutes.** 6,000 steps is about 0.2B tokens and 35 minutes on the Spark; the recipe followed (Samuel et al. 2023) trained on about 131B. Every arm was still improving when it stopped. *(24 Sep)*

## What the text needs

11. **Text chosen to read like rai reads rai-like text better.** At matched steps the 40M selection had lower held-out loss on rai-like rows than the 75M (5.79 against 6.01 at 4,500 steps). *(24 Sep)*
12. ~~**Less has been more predictable, and so far more.**~~ Overturned at 6,000 steps (24 Sep, 21:28): less was steadier but not more. See 12a. The 40M arm rose at every point (5, 30, 37, 73); the 75M arm went 15, 0, 38, 48 with better honest margins but worse non-answers. One run each; the full-corpus arm and a second seed decide it. *(24 Sep)*
12a. **Selecting text by likeness to the training rows buys the familiar forms and costs the new ones.** At 6,000 steps the 40M selection led on v1, the forms rai trains on (53 against 45), and the whole corpus led on artwork (11 against 5) and unseen forms (27 against 15), for totals of 73 and 83, a tie. Choose text for the reader's reach, not only its likeness to what it will be trained on, and read the unseen set first. *(24 Sep, 21:28)*
13. **The prose closest to rai is the scarcest.** Selection kept Gutenberg almost whole (97% at 75M, 80% at 40M) and Hansard hardly at all (17%, 2%). A bigger Gutenberg pool is the next lever for text. *(24 Sep)*
13a. **A small selection cannot carry a long run alone; mix it.** The long run needs about 60,000 steps, which is some 35 passes over the 40M selection. Repeating text costs almost nothing up to about 4 passes and then decays (Muennighoff et al. 2023); well-filtered text holds up to about 10 (Fang et al. 2025); mixed with general text, target text can be reused 15–20 times (Sedova et al. 2026). So `mom-mix-1` draws half of each batch from the selection and half from the whole corpus, reading the selection about 15 times in 48,000 steps. The whole-corpus arm's first two points (22 at 1,500, 17 at 3,000, margins −15.0 and −6.6) repeat the 75M pattern: better margins than the selection, jumpier counts. *(24 Sep, about 21:15)*

13b. **The mix carried furthest.** `mom-mix-1` (half the 40M selection, half the whole corpus) counted 77 at 3,000 steps, where the arms had counted 30, 0 and 17, and reached 151 at 18,000 (104, 15, 32). *(25 Sep, 01:00)*
13c. **What stops the reader now is knowing what things are.** In rounds 6–8 the same off-question answer set the threshold (*"Which website or app was it?" / "The cancellation charge for a waitlisted ticket."*, at 7.3, 15.4, 10.0), with v1's worst of the same kind (*"Where was the queue?" / "Looked at my phone."* → *my phone*). Grammar says each is a thing of the asked kind; only knowing that a charge is not an app, or a phone not a place, says otherwise. Honest answers on unseen forms sit at a median margin near +5 against +12 to +16 on trained forms. Pointing is learned; knowledge is short. *(25 Sep, 01:00)*
13d. **A run stopped by its stall rule stops before its learning rate has come down.** `mom-mix-1` stopped at step 24,000 of a decay planned over 48,000, at half the peak rate. Either decay over the steps a run will actually take, or finish the decay after a stall before judging the base. *(25 Sep, 01:00)*

13e. ~~**Finishing the decay paid.**~~ Not shown: the anneal's rounds went 103, 158, 101, so 158 was one good draw, not the anneal's effect (25 Sep, about 05:00). See 5a. `mom-mix-1`, stopped at 134 halfway down its learning rate, counted 158 (108, 16, 34) after two of three rounds bringing the rate to zero, above its best of 151 at full rate. When rai is trained again on `mom`, the fillers, forms and test answers need authors that are not such a model — people, or code drawing words from open sources. `mom`'s own pointing rows already qualify, since they are cut from the open corpus. *(25 Sep)*

13h. **At batch 256 a peak rate of 5e-4 throws a base about.** `mom-big-1` scored 130, 92 and 71 (seed means where measured) in rounds 3–5 at the peak rate, the base worsening while the text was new. Four times the batch and a moving average of the weights are the fix in `mom-big-2`. *(25 Sep, about 07:27)*
13i. **Matching token types to how the reader reads raises every margin and not the count.** Without token types the honest answers' median margins rose from about 6 to 14–18, and the worst non-answers rose as much or more: 114 against 130. What sets the count is the tail of confident non-answers, and pointing settings alone do not reach it. *(25 Sep, before 07:25)*

13j. **Four times the batch at the same schedule learns four times slower in updates.** `mom-big-2` (batch 1,024) scored 0 on every seed after 1,000 steps, while `mom-big-1` (batch 256) had made 3,000 updates by the same text. Steadiness belongs to the rate and the averaging, not the batch. *(25 Sep, 08:05)*
13k. **Score a model mid-run through a stand-in for the decay.** At a high steady rate the weights oscillate and only a decay shows progress (Wen et al. 2024); a moving average of the weights stands in for it (Tian et al. 2025). Scoring the raw weights at the peak rate measured the swing. *(25 Sep, 08:05)*
13l. **Do not start again from zero when a good base exists.** Continuing from a checkpoint with a short re-warm and a re-decay matches retraining (Gupta et al. 2023; Ibrahim et al. 2024). `mom-big-3` carries on from `mom-big-1`'s round 3. *(25 Sep, 08:20)*

13m. **Teach the line between an answer and a plausible non-answer with data made for it.** What stops the clean base is that line (13c), and SQuAD 2.0 was built to teach it: 43,498 unanswerable questions written to look answerable. Likely reason: it teaches finding an answer inside a long Wikipedia paragraph, where rai reads a person's short answer to their own question, a different line between answer and non-answer. The clean base also needs rai's rows at full strength to learn rai's forms. Not tuned further (lesson 0); admission stands for a later base. *(25 Sep, 09:05; results 10:23)* **Checked and souped (10:55):** the rows were sound (the answer text appears first somewhere other than the marked spot in 3.8% of answerable questions, nearly always the same words earlier in the passage; 0.1% of answers are cut off at 384 tokens). Souped (13o), the harsh pairing counted 134 against 129 for the same base without SQuAD, a tie; the gentle one 37, its honest answers' median margins below zero.

13r. **The clean base has read its corpus once; the recipe it follows reads its corpus a thousand times.** LTG-BERT (Samuel et al. 2023) trains 31,250 steps of 4M tokens on 131M subwords, so every token is seen about 1,000 times; half that many steps scored the same, a quarter (about 250 passes) scored noticeably worse. `mom-big-1` and `mom-big-3` together have made 24,000 steps of 32K tokens, 0.77B tokens over an 833M-token corpus: less than one pass. Lesson 13a's four-pass ceiling (Muennighoff et al. 2023) was measured on decoder models, whose targets repeat exactly; a masked model draws new masks each pass. At the Spark's present pace (3,000 steps in about 40 minutes, 3.5B tokens a day) 250 passes is two months, so the pace of pretraining itself, tokens an hour, is a lever worth measuring before more schedule changes. **Fresh text, measured (25 Sep, 15:56):** `mom-big-4`, carried on from round 3 of `mom-big-3` but reading windows past the ones that run had read (a window offset of 16,750 steps), dropped the held-out loss from 5.753 to 5.588 in one round of 3,000 steps, more than any round before it, and counted 185 (71, 159, 159) with the clean rows, the clean base's best. The pace alone is GPU-bound (about 2 steps a second at 96%); the text it reads is the lever, which is why the wider base gets a corpus four times the size (13x). *(25 Sep, 13:05; 15:56)*

13s. **Judge a big run's progress by the held-out loss as well as the count, and go back to the best base only when neither moved.** The count is one confident non-answer's draw (1, 13p): rounds 3, 4 and 5 of `mom-big-3` read alike and counted 176, 124, 122. Under the count alone the run stopped at round 5, and its rule of carrying on from the best base sent every round back to round 3, so pretraining could never accumulate past it: a treadmill on a base that has read its corpus once (13r). From 25 Sep, 13:20 `clean_base.py --big` counts a round as progress if it sets a new best count or lowers the held-out loss (masked-LM loss on rai-like rows under the moving average, which the corpus cannot teach by heart) below the round before; two rounds with neither stop the run, as before; a round that counted less but learned carries on, and only a round with neither goes back to the best base, which is now written to `latest` so a resumed run does not repeat it. The steady rate's re-warm raises the held-out loss for a round (5.753 at round 3, 5.775 at round 5), so a change of rate is read after the dip (lesson 0). Restarted from round 5's weights, `--max-rounds 15`. *(25 Sep, 13:20)*

13t. **The pointing stage's rate schedule does not reach the count either.** Round 3's base, pointed three ways and souped: constant 1e-4 for 5 passes (the recipe) 176; warm-up and linear decay to zero at a 1e-4 peak, 151, its medians on trained forms lower (14.7, 15.3 against 16.3, 18.4) because the average rate halved (13m, 13q); the same schedule at a 2e-4 peak, the constant recipe's average rate, 131 with the same medians as the recipe (16.0, 18.1, 7.9) and the threshold at 16.19 on the off-question answer of 13c. Mosbach et al. 2021's warm-up, decay and longer training steady a fine-tuning whose seeds fail outright; rai's seeds all learn, and their swing is the tail of confident non-answers, which the schedule's shape does not move (13i). Longer pointing scales every margin up together, as token types did (13i); read a median only against its threshold, and let the count decide. Constant 1e-4 for 5 passes stays; the 2e-4 constant variant was cancelled unmeasured. *(25 Sep, 13:20; completed 14:15)*

## Loop or hook

Facts come from a **hook**: the end of each round, inside the training script, which writes `state.json`. Rounds are the natural points of rest, so nothing needs to poll. Insights do not come from a hook, because deciding what a number means is judgement. They are written here at each point of rest by whoever is present, and read before the next run is designed. A **loop** (checking on a timer) fits neither: it adds checks where nothing has happened and cannot write a lesson.

*Lessons about running training on our own machines (the Spark, the sessions that drive it, the watches and the hook that enforces this file) are kept in `GRAPEVINE-OPS.md`, which is not released.*

13z98. **A loss that matches the reading learns the training texts by heart before it learns the writer (gagar, 6 November 2026).** 13z97 said order shows on this base only as a comparison of two arrangements. Trained for that comparison directly (a pairwise margin on a window against its own move), the window reader learned the ranking the pointwise loss never had: 72%, 83%, 88% of training pairs right by epoch, 93% at six epochs, against a pointwise loss that sat at chance. The kept windows did not follow: 54 and 15 at three epochs against 55 and 18 before, then 50 and 21 at six. With about 750 windows of the writer's among the rows, the reader memorises those windows' own swaps before it learns what a swap is in his prose, and more passes make it worse; three seeds souped help a little because they disagree about what they memorised. That the bound is the amount of the writer's text, not the recipe, is an inference from two epoch counts on one seed (Hermes's reading, 12:14): the negatives being each window's own turnings fits the same numbers, and only a learning curve on half the writer's windows at the same recipe would make it a finding. Two more from the same round: a prediction objective must pose the question the reading asks (a next-line reader judged among the whole batch learned which text a line belongs to and nothing of its place; judged among its own text's lines it learned nothing at all from 3,469 texts: accuracy exactly at chance, which marks a training that has not learned rather than a base that cannot, since the same base's frozen vectors show the residual falling through a text in order); and timing on a shared runner is read from the per-text minimum over runs, never from one run (the same engine measured 0.47 to 1.01 of torch across four runs on one CPU model; the minima put ONNX Runtime fp32 at 0.53 with every answer the same). *(6 November 2026, 10:55)*

13z99. **A per-pair saving does not pay a per-text cost on every text; a cheaper order check is measured by the band it leaves and by the shortest text it reads (gagar, 6 November 2026).** The order check's estimate is paid per sentence pair, with a floor at the neighbours; a whole-text comparison that stops a halves swap is paid per text, about 0.35 s however short the text. A `step` on six of twelve layers halved the estimate and sent 24 of 64 windows to the full check where twelve layers send 9, so 17 texts got slower, the worst by three times, and it lost 9 in-order windows besides. The same `step` reading 2n sampled pairs instead of 4n saved 30% at the median and still left 16 of 64 in-order texts slower: six the weaker estimate sent to the full check, at five times the cost, and ten short texts whose estimate was already a few batches. No sample size meets "no text slower" with a per-text read added, since the shortest texts pay the most. What can: removing per-text work (the full check), or an engine that cuts every pair by more than the reads cost on the shortest text; and an engine's per-text timing read on twelve windows of 17 to 24 sentences says nothing yet about a five-sentence text. *(6 November 2026, 12:13)*

13z100. **Negatives narrowed to the comparison a reader is used for make a more confident and worse comparator (gagar, 6 November 2026).** `arc2` is served only to compare a text with its own turning, but three of its four negatives were other moves. Trained on turnings alone (`arc3`, the same recipe otherwise), the reader read the writer's windows in order against halves swapped at 49 and 14 where `arc2` gives 53 and 8, with a median P(written) of 0.95 in order and 0.002 swapped against 0.71 and 0.03, and an in-order window beat its own swap on 50 of 64 against 55. The other negatives were not diluting the comparison; they were holding the reader to the text. A smaller negative space helps a reader that has too much to learn from, and this one had too little. *(6 November 2026, 12:13)*

13z101. **A reader is read under the rule that will be served, against negatives made by more than one recipe; two rules on one reader gave two verdicts (gagar, 6 November 2026).** Under one cut, ungated, `arc2`'s soup reads 55 in order and 14 swapped; under both middle cuts after clear-then-full, which is what the server runs, 53 and 8; a pairwise reader "closed on generalisation" against the first bar was within one of `arc2` under the second. And every swap in the test windows had been made at n // 2, so a one-cut rule at (n + 1) // 2 looked as good as both cuts by construction, until a second swap at the other cut was made for every text: each one-cut rule is blind to the swap the other cut makes (55 / 14 / 7 and 56 / 9 / 14 against 53 / 8 / 5), the third read is the price of not knowing where a pasted text was cut, and only odd texts pay it. The served rule's loss falls on them too: 8 of the 20 odd windows lost against 3 of 44 even, and 9 of the 11 lost are cuts from the middle of a page. *(6 November 2026, 12:13)*

13z102. **Under a runtime that merges adapters every adapter is a full copy of the base, so the time an engine buys is paid in memory; and the longest times are made in the full check (gagar, 6 November 2026).** On x86 ONNX Runtime fp32 halved the order check with every answer the same, read on the per-text minima over three runs of a shared runner; beside torch it added about 350 MB of RSS, and with every reader under it alone, 887 MB at load against torch's 446, because two LoRA adapters of a few MB each became two 127 MiB graphs. On a box with 611 MB free that is the whole budget; the memory-neutral form, adapters unmerged inside one graph, is a build and not a measurement. The one place an app's longest times are made is the full check, every ordered pair at 40 words and 1,000 draws for the texts the estimate leaves uncertain. With the estimate's free forwards-over-backwards test stopping every reversal and one 512-token read of a whole-text reader stopping shuffles (0 of 64 against the full check's 4), the longest of forty posts fell from 10.7 s to 2.4 with the marks unchanged, at 61 of 64 of the writer's windows against a line of 62: read as the worst-case lever, not adopted. *(6 November 2026, 12:13)*

13z103. **Layers of small models are not lighter than the one larger model they replace until each layer is costed; a finder and a pair-reading pointer outweigh it (Coherence v2, 11 November 2026).** Measured on gpubox CPU at 4 threads, minimum of 15 runs: v1's one DeBERTa classifier loads 373 MB and reads concept 041 in 126 ms; v2 as specced (a 33M reader with every pointer head on it, plus the bge-base finder) loads 618 MB and takes 447 ms, 1,005 ms at eight sentences, and with each pointer a separately fine-tuned copy 827 MB at the same speed. Most of the memory is the finder, and most of the time is the rests-on pointer reading every claim against every sentence, which grows with the square of the sentence count. *Small* by the part a model plays (2 October 2026) is not small by what the server carries: before saying a layered design is lighter, load every layer in one process and time the worst case, and count the outside call the old design made (v1's Haiku), which this measurement could not. *(11 November 2026, 10:05)*

13z104. **A classifier trained to grade a whole text does not carry, frozen, the relation between two of its sentences; a small reader fine-tuned for the relation does, and a cue list sits between them (Coherence v2 synthetic probe, 11 November 2026).** On 3,000 code-assembled concepts (test fifth on templates never trained on; 1,250 test claims; none of it his keys), "which sentence does this claim rest on, or none": a cue list 0.63 (0.48 on claims that do rest on something, every unsupported claim right because its templates carry no connective); word overlap 0.43; RAM's 33M base fine-tuned as a pair reader 0.99, 0.98, 0.99 over seeds 7, 1, 2; v1's DeBERTa frozen with one linear head on the pair CLS 0.37, 0.35, 0.38, below the cue list, its features tuned to five whole-concept dimensions and blind to which sentence holds which. The 0.99 says the task and the plumbing work and nothing about real prose: the idea forms are a closed bank the reader meets in training under other templates. The frozen result is the lesson: a head on a frozen encoder inherits what the encoder was trained to see, so a frozen base is chosen for the relation it must read, and shown on held-out keys before it is kept. *(11 November 2026, 10:15)*

13z105. **A sentence-embedding encoder frozen under a head half-learns a relation between sentences and fails it outright when the two sentences are fed to it as one pair; fine-tuned, the cheap head over two sentence vectors matches reading every pair (Coherence v2, 11 November 2026).** Same synthetic probe as 13z104, on bge-small (33M): frozen, a head on [a, b, a·b, |a−b|] of two sentence vectors 0.69, 0.65, 0.71 over seeds 7, 1, 2 (0.56 on supported claims, just above the cue list's 0.48); frozen as a pair reader with a linear head on the pair's CLS 0.10, 0.10, 0.09, below chance, because the encoder never saw a pair and its CLS of one carries no relation; fine-tuned with the two-vector head 0.98, 0.97, 0.99 (every supported claim right, the unsupported at 0.92); fine-tuned as a pair reader 0.98, 0.99, 0.99. 13z106. **The interpreter is the weight: an engine that must stay under 100 MB cannot be in Python, and a C process on ONNX Runtime holds at 81 MB with the heap trimmed after each request (Coherence v2, 11 November 2026).** Python with numpy, onnxruntime and tokenizers imported is 65 MB before any model; the same int8 bge-small engine (32 MB of weights) in Python is 139 MB loaded and 219 MB after 50 requests, 139 of them the interpreter and the allocator's keepings. 13z107. **Judge a quantised encoder by a test with a right answer, never by whether its best pick agrees with fp32; and the smaller encoder is a step below the larger one on exactly the finder's task (Coherence v2, 11 November 2026).** bge-base at fp32, activation-int8, weight-only int8 and 4-bit scores 0.895, 0.892, 0.893, 0.893 on STS-B, while its best emoji pick among 1,325 captions agrees with fp32 only 850f the time, because near-ties flip under any change; the fp32 pick stays in the quantised top five 98 to 990f the time. bge-small: STS-B 0.889 (0.887 to 0.889 quantised), half a point under base, but 0.54 against 0.61 on retrieval over 72 short paraphrased idea phrasings, which is the finder's own kind of question, and 0.47 to 0.53 quantised.
